## 4. Studying with AI
**[SCENE: Screen + Cam. Fresh AI chat open.]**

Now here's where it gets fun. The planner comes with fifty AI prompts I use to study. Let me show you two of my favorites.

This first one is my go-to when a topic just isn't clicking. I'll use DHCP as the example.

**[Type prompt #01 and send:]**
> Explain DHCP like I'm a smart beginner studying for CompTIA A+. Use one real-world analogy, then give me the technical version in 5 bullet points.

**[While it loads:]** The trick is asking for an analogy first, and then the technical version. You get the intuition, and then the details stick to it.

**[Read the analogy out loud, then say one of these, whichever is true:]**
- "Okay, that actually makes it click."
- "I didn't know that part about [detail]. That's going in my notes."

**[New chat or keep going. Type prompt #26 and send:]**
> Give me a realistic help-desk ticket about a user who can't connect to the office Wi-Fi. Let me ask questions and choose steps, and respond like the user would. Don't reveal the cause until I solve it.

This one turns the AI into a frustrated user, so I can practice troubleshooting. Which, remember, is the biggest part of Core 1.

**[Play 3–4 turns. Think out loud as you type, for example:]**
- "First thing I'd ask: is it just them, or is the whole office down?"
- "Okay, let me check whether they're getting an IP address at all."

**[When you solve it, or give up:]**
- "There it is. That's exactly the kind of question that shows up on the exam."
- or: "Okay, I didn't get that one. Which is exactly why I'm practicing."

One important thing. AI can be confidently wrong, so I always double-check against the official exam objectives. And never use real exam questions or braindumps. That breaks CompTIA's rules and can cost you the certification. Everything here creates original practice.

**[PAUSE, then cut]**
