How to use this planner

A+ is two exams. You pass Core 1 and Core 2 separately and need both. This planner splits your time the same way.

The plan in one picture

WeeksFocusMilestone
1–4Core 1: mobile devices, networking, hardware, virtualization and cloud, hardware and network troubleshootingTake Core 1 once your practice scores are ready
5–7Core 2: operating systems, security, software troubleshooting, operational proceduresPractice scores at or above your target
8Full practice exams, weak spots, exam-day prepTake Core 2 (or both, if you waited)

Pick your pace. Sprint both exams in 8 weeks (90+ min a day), or do what I'm doing: one exam at a time, with a buffer week before each. That's about 10–12 weeks total.

The daily rhythm (60–90 minutes)

BlockTimeWhat you do
Learn20–30 minWatch or read one objective (a video series, book chapter, or course module)
Explain15–20 minWrite notes in your own words. Use an AI prompt from the prompts section to clear up anything fuzzy
Drill15–20 minFlashcards plus 10–20 practice questions on today's topic
Review10 minRe-read what you missed. Add it to your weak-spot list

The weekly rhythm

  • One hands-on lab (build, configure, break, or fix something real or virtual)
  • One timed practice quiz; log the score in the tracker
  • One review session on your weak-spot list before starting the next week
  • One day off. Rest is part of the plan
Weeks 1–4

Core 1 (220-1201)

Check off each topic when you can explain it without notes.

Week 1 · Mobile devices and networking foundationsDates: ____________
  • Laptop hardware: replacing RAM, storage, batteries, keyboards, screens
  • Mobile display types and components (digitizer, Wi-Fi antenna, webcam)
  • Mobile accessories, ports, and connection methods (USB-C, NFC, Bluetooth, hotspot)
  • Mobile device management, sync, and email configuration
  • TCP vs. UDP and the common ports and protocols
  • Networking hardware: routers, switches (managed/unmanaged), access points, firewalls, PoE
  • OSI model basics as a troubleshooting map
  • Lab: drill ports daily with the free A+ Trainer

Goal: every port and protocol answered correctly in Port → Protocol and Protocol → Port modes.

Week 2 · Networking in depthDates: ____________
  • Wireless standards (802.11 a/b/g/n/ac/ax), frequencies, and channels
  • Wireless security: WPA2, WPA3, and enterprise authentication (RADIUS)
  • Networked host services: DNS, DHCP, file/print, web, mail, authentication, syslog
  • DNS record types and DHCP scopes, leases, and reservations
  • IPv4 vs. IPv6, private vs. public addresses, APIPA, static vs. dynamic
  • SOHO router configuration: NAT, port forwarding, DMZ, firmware updates
  • Internet connection types and network types (LAN, WAN, PAN, MAN, WLAN)
  • Networking tools: crimper, cable tester, toner probe, loopback plug, Wi-Fi analyzer

Goal: configure (or simulate) a home router from scratch and explain every setting.

Week 3 · HardwareDates: ____________
  • Cables and connectors: network, video, peripheral, storage
  • RAM types, channels, and installation
  • Storage: HDD vs. SSD, interfaces (SATA, NVMe, M.2), and RAID levels 0/1/5/10
  • Motherboard form factors, expansion slots, BIOS/UEFI settings, TPM, Secure Boot
  • CPUs, cooling, and power supplies (wattage, rails, modular vs. non-modular)
  • Peripherals and multifunction devices
  • Printers: laser imaging process, inkjet, thermal, impact; maintenance and sharing
  • Lab: open a PC (or watch a teardown) and name every component

Goal: recite the laser printing steps in order and pick the right RAID level for a scenario.

Week 4 · Virtualization, cloud, and troubleshootingDates: ____________
  • Virtualization concepts: hypervisor types, VM requirements, sandboxing
  • Cloud models (public, private, hybrid, community) and IaaS / PaaS / SaaS
  • Cloud characteristics: shared resources, elasticity, metered use, availability
  • The CompTIA troubleshooting methodology (all steps, in order)
  • Troubleshooting motherboards, RAM, CPU, power, and storage/RAID
  • Troubleshooting displays, mobile devices, and printers
  • Troubleshooting wired and wireless network problems
  • Two full Core 1 practice exams under timed conditions

Goal: practice scores consistently above your target. If so, book Core 1.

Weeks 5–8

Core 2 (220-1202)

Core 2 is about operating systems, security, and how IT work gets done. Expect more scenario questions.

Week 5 · Operating systemsDates: ____________
  • Windows editions and their feature differences
  • Installation and upgrade methods, boot methods, partitioning, file systems
  • Command-line tools (ipconfig, ping, tracert, netstat, chkdsk, sfc, diskpart, gpupdate, and more)
  • Windows tools: Task Manager, MMC snap-ins, Event Viewer, Disk Management, Registry Editor
  • Settings and Control Panel utilities
  • Windows networking: workgroups vs. domains, shares, mapped drives, firewall
  • macOS and Linux basics: features, tools, and common commands
  • Lab: install Windows and a Linux distro in VMs and try every tool on the list

Goal: given a problem, name the exact tool or command you would use first.

Week 6 · SecurityDates: ____________
  • Physical security measures and logical security concepts
  • Authentication: MFA, SSO, least privilege, access control lists
  • Wireless security protocols and authentication methods
  • Malware types and how to detect, remove, and prevent them
  • Social engineering attacks and other threats and vulnerabilities
  • Windows security settings: Defender, firewall, BitLocker, user accounts, UAC
  • Securing workstations, mobile devices, and SOHO networks
  • Data destruction and disposal methods; browser security settings

Goal: walk through the malware removal steps in order without notes.

Week 7 · Software troubleshooting and operational proceduresDates: ____________
  • Troubleshooting Windows OS problems (boot issues, slow performance, crashes)
  • Troubleshooting mobile OS, app, and security issues
  • Troubleshooting common PC security issues
  • Documentation, ticketing, asset management, and change management
  • Backup and recovery methods (full, incremental, differential, 3-2-1)
  • Safety procedures, environmental controls, and proper disposal
  • Privacy, licensing, and policy concepts; professional communication
  • Scripting basics and remote access technologies

Goal: explain a change-management process and a backup strategy for a small office.

Week 8 · Final review and exam dayDates: ____________
  • Full Core 2 practice exam #1 (timed)
  • Review every missed question and its objective
  • Weak-spot sprint: your lowest two domains
  • Full Core 2 practice exam #2 (timed)
  • Practice performance-based questions (PBQs)
  • Quick review: ports, troubleshooting steps, malware removal steps
  • Exam logistics confirmed (see the exam-day checklist)
  • Light review only the day before. Sleep.

Goal: walk in calm, rested, and already familiar with the question styles.

50 AI study prompts

Study smarter with AI

Copy a prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any AI assistant. Replace anything in [brackets].

Two rules. First, AI can be confidently wrong, so check anything important against the official objectives or your study materials. Second, never ask for (or share) real exam questions. Using braindumps breaks CompTIA's candidate agreement and can cost you the certification. Everything here generates original practice.

1. Understand a concept (prompts 1–10)

  1. 01Explain [topic] like I'm a smart beginner studying for CompTIA A+. Use one real-world analogy, then give me the technical version in 5 bullet points.
  2. 02What's the difference between [concept A] and [concept B]? Give me a comparison table and one scenario where choosing the wrong one causes a problem.
  3. 03I'm studying A+ objective [objective number and name]. List every term I need to know for it, with a one-sentence definition for each.
  4. 04Walk me through what happens step by step when [process, e.g. a computer requests a web page]. Number each step.
  5. 05I think [my explanation of a topic]. Is that correct? Point out anything wrong or missing, gently but clearly.
  6. 06Teach me [topic] using the Feynman technique: explain it simply, then ask me to explain it back, then correct my gaps.
  7. 07Why does [technology] exist? What problem did it solve, and what came before it?
  8. 08Give me the 5 most common misconceptions students have about [topic], and the correct understanding for each.
  9. 09Explain [topic] at three levels: a one-sentence summary, a one-paragraph explanation, and a deep dive for someone who wants to work in IT.
  10. 10Create a simple text diagram showing how [components or network devices] connect and how data flows between them.

2. Memorize it (prompts 11–17)

  1. 11Create 20 flashcards for [topic] in "front | back" format so I can import them into Anki or Quizlet.
  2. 12Give me 3 memorable mnemonics for [list to memorize, e.g. the troubleshooting steps]. Make at least one funny.
  3. 13Turn these ports and protocols into a short story I'll remember: [list].
  4. 14Quiz me on [topic] one question at a time. Wait for my answer, tell me if I'm right, explain briefly, then ask the next one. Increase difficulty as I get answers right.
  5. 15Make a one-page cheat sheet for [domain] with only the facts most likely to appear on the exam.
  6. 16Here are the topics I keep forgetting: [list]. Build me a 7-day spaced-repetition review schedule for them.
  7. 17Group these terms into categories that make them easier to remember, and explain the logic of each group: [list of terms].

3. Practice questions (prompts 18–25)

  1. 18Write 10 original multiple-choice practice questions on [objective] in the style of CompTIA A+. Don't show the answers until I reply with mine.
  2. 19Write 5 "BEST answer" questions on [topic] where more than one option is technically possible, and explain why the best one wins.
  3. 20I got this practice question wrong: [paste question, my answer, correct answer]. Explain why my answer was wrong and how to spot the right one next time.
  4. 21Give me 10 "which tool or command would you use?" questions for Windows troubleshooting.
  5. 22Create a 20-question mixed review covering [list of domains], weighted toward [my weakest domain].
  6. 23Write 5 questions with long, wordy scenarios on [topic] so I can practice finding the important details in exam-style questions.
  7. 24Act as a strict exam proctor. Give me a timed 15-question quiz on [domain], then grade me and tell me which objectives to review.
  8. 25For each of these wrong answer choices, explain why it's wrong so I learn the distractors too: [paste question and options].

4. Troubleshooting scenarios and PBQs (prompts 26–33)

  1. 26Give me a realistic help-desk ticket about [hardware / network / OS problem]. Let me ask questions and choose steps, and respond like the user would. Don't reveal the cause until I solve it.
  2. 27Walk me through the CompTIA troubleshooting methodology using this problem: [symptom]. Pause at each step and let me decide what to do.
  3. 28Create a performance-based practice task: give me a scenario, a list of items, and ask me to match or order them correctly. Topic: [e.g. cable types, RAID levels, laser printing steps].
  4. 29A user reports [symptom]. List the 5 most likely causes from most to least likely, and the quickest test for each.
  5. 30Simulate a Windows command prompt. I'll type commands to diagnose [network problem], and you show realistic output.
  6. 31Give me a SOHO network scenario with something misconfigured. Describe the router settings and let me find the mistake.
  7. 32Create a malware-infection scenario and quiz me on the correct removal steps in order.
  8. 33Here's how I'd solve this ticket: [my steps]. Grade it like a senior technician would, and tell me what I skipped.

5. Hands-on labs (prompts 34–39)

  1. 34Design a free home lab for practicing [topic] using virtual machines and whatever old hardware I have: [list hardware].
  2. 35Give me a step-by-step lab to practice [skill, e.g. partitioning a drive, setting up a file share] in a Windows virtual machine.
  3. 36Write a 30-minute lab exploring these Linux commands, with a challenge at the end: [list of commands].
  4. 37I want to break and fix something safely to learn [topic]. Suggest 3 "break it" exercises I can do in a VM, and how to recover from each.
  5. 38Create a checklist for setting up and securing a new SOHO router, and explain why each step matters.
  6. 39Explain what each setting does on this screen and what I should set it to for [goal]: [describe or paste the settings].

6. Plan and review (prompts 40–45)

  1. 40My exam is on [date] and I can study [X] minutes a day. Build me a day-by-day plan covering all A+ Core [1 or 2] domains, weighted by exam percentage.
  2. 41Here are my practice scores by domain: [scores]. Tell me where to spend my next 7 days and why.
  3. 42Turn my messy notes into a clean, organized study guide with headings and bullets: [paste notes].
  4. 43Summarize this video transcript or chapter into the key exam facts, then give me 5 questions to check that I understood it: [paste text].
  5. 44I'm behind schedule by [X days]. Help me adjust my plan without skipping anything heavily weighted.
  6. 45At the end of each week, ask me 10 questions about what I studied and tell me if I'm ready to move on.

7. Exam strategy and mindset (prompts 46–50)

  1. 46What are the best strategies for answering CompTIA-style multiple-choice and performance-based questions? Include time management.
  2. 47Teach me how to spot keywords like BEST, FIRST, and MOST LIKELY in exam questions, with 5 practice examples.
  3. 48Give me a 24-hour pre-exam checklist: what to review, what to skip, and what to do the morning of.
  4. 49I'm nervous about my exam. Help me make a short, practical plan for staying calm during the test.
  5. 50I just passed (or didn't pass) A+. Help me plan what's next: review weak areas for a retake, or map a path to Network+ and Security+.
